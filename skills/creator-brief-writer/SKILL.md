# Creator Brief Writer

## Purpose

Turn product information or an existing campaign brief into an execution-ready creator brief. The brief must connect four layers:

1. business objective and audience;
2. product positioning, proof points, and claims;
3. creator-facing content direction and shot requirements;
4. publishing, review, rights, and measurement rules.

When the user gives an existing brief, first extract its structure and distinguish hard requirements from examples, suggestions, and missing fields. Do not blindly copy wording or unsupported claims.

## Operating modes

Choose the mode that matches the request:

- **Deconstruct**: map an existing brief into sections, fields, intent, dependencies, and gaps. End with a reusable framework.
- **Create**: write a new brief from product, audience, channel, market, budget, timing, and creator information.
- **Adapt**: convert a brief between photography, short video, long video, KOL, KOC, UGC, affiliate, or multi-platform formats.
- **Audit**: identify ambiguity, unverifiable claims, missing shot proof, conflicting deadlines, weak CTAs, and rights/approval risks.

If the request is ambiguous, infer the smallest reasonable mode from context and state assumptions in a short “Assumptions” section.

## Default brief architecture

Use this order unless the user asks for another format:

1. **Project basics** — campaign name, brand, product/SKU, market, creator type, platform, publication window, contact, and status.
2. **Campaign objective** — one primary goal (awareness, consideration, conversion, or launch support), secondary goals, KPI direction, and CTA.
3. **Audience and insight** — who the content is for, their problem, current workaround, and the reason they may care now.
4. **Product positioning** — one-sentence positioning, category, problem solved, three to five key selling points, proof/evidence, and claim limits.
5. **Content direction** — recommended hook, story angle, format, tone, creator POV, opening/middle/ending beats, and what must be shown on camera.
6. **Required scenarios / shot list** — each item must specify the action, proof to capture, and the intended message. Use “must-have” and “optional” labels.
7. **Deliverables and specs** — platform, duration, aspect ratio, resolution, language, subtitles, title/description/hashtags/tags, links, coupon code, and file naming.
8. **Brand and compliance rules** — logo/package visibility, disclosures, prohibited claims, privacy/safety constraints, and local platform rules.
9. **Workflow** — concept/script approval, sample delivery, draft review, revision rounds, publication, usage rights, whitelisting/paid usage, and final asset handoff.
10. **Measurement and acceptance** — tracking link/code, reporting window, required screenshots/data, acceptance checklist, and owner for each approval.

## How to write each talking point

For every selling point, write the following chain:

`user problem → product capability → concrete proof/number → creator action or shot → viewer takeaway`

Keep product claims exactly as supplied. If a number, compatibility statement, discount, deadline, or performance claim is not verified, mark it **待验证** instead of inventing or upgrading it.

Translate technical features into observable demonstrations. For example, “fast transfer” should become a defined file upload, comparison setup, and visible result; “AI album assistant” should become a screen recording showing classification and selection.

## Non-negotiable quality rules

- Separate **Must-have**, **Recommended**, **Optional**, and **Do not** items.
- Do not confuse product positioning with a list of features. State why the feature matters to this audience.
- Every mandatory claim needs either a supplied proof source, a demonstrable shot, or a **待验证** label.
- Do not promise results such as views, sales, ROI, or platform distribution unless the user supplies a target and measurement method.
- Make the CTA executable: tell the viewer where to click, what code to use, and where the link appears.
- Make timing operational: include the review buffer, revision deadline, publication window, and who approves.
- Make usage rights explicit: organic reposting, paid usage, whitelisting, duration, territory, and editing permission.
- Keep the brief concise enough for a creator to use during production. Put long background explanations in an appendix or reference section.

## Output format

For a **deconstruction**, output:

1. one-paragraph summary of the brief’s strategy;
2. a field-by-field table: `原文信息 | 作用 | 类型（硬要求/建议/示例/待补） | 写作注意`;
3. the reusable framework;
4. missing information and risk list;
5. a clean blank template.

For a **new or adapted brief**, output the full brief in the default architecture above, followed by:

- creator-facing one-page checklist;
- brand review checklist;
- assumptions and **待验证** items;
- optional bilingual wording only when requested.
